Case study: fixing a prior-authorization bottleneck

Capstone project · HealthTech Business Analysis programme

Kestrel Health Plan was the case organisation for my capstone. The figures below come from that scenario, and the outcomes are design targets, not live results.

The problem

Prior-authorization (PA) requests arrived by phone, fax and email, then were re-keyed into spreadsheets and emailed to nurses. Routine decisions took 26 hours against a 2-hour target, surgeries and scans were rescheduled, and employer renewals were at risk.

My contribution

I produced the capstone deliverable: a six-item gap register, Five Whys and fishbone root-cause analysis, AS-IS and TO-BE swimlane maps, a prioritised set of gaps with KPIs, and a business requirements document (BRD).

Approach and tools

Gap analysis, Five Whys, Ishikawa diagram, swimlane process mapping, KPI definition, functional and non-functional requirements written as Given/When/Then acceptance criteria, and MoSCoW prioritisation with a traceability matrix linking each requirement to a gap and a stakeholder need.

Key finding and outcome

The root cause was a fragmented, manual workflow, not a missing portal. I recommended structured intake, an automated rules engine, a prioritised nurse queue, automated alerts and claims linking in Phase 1, and deferred a vendor provider portal to Phase 2. The deliverable sets targets such as 95% of decisions within 2 hours and pended claims down from 21% to under 2%.

How I work

The way I approach digital health problems, set out as case-study panels. The diagrams are illustrative of my method, not client work.

Seeing the workflow as it is really done

The clinical reality
Care runs on handovers, checks and records. The process on paper rarely matches what happens at the bedside.
My approach
Map the current process with the people who do it, mark where information is repeated, delayed or lost, then redesign around safe practice.
What I bring
Clinical workflow knowledge from hospital and care-home settings, plus process mapping in Visio and Lucidchart.

Defining the distance between today and the target

The clinical reality
Change lands on busy teams. A new system that ignores how people work adds steps instead of removing them.
My approach
Describe the current state, agree the target state with stakeholders, and make the gap, and its impact on staff, explicit before delivery starts.
What I bring
Gap analysis, change impact assessment, root cause analysis and clinical workflow redesign.

Requirements that survive delivery

The clinical reality
A system can meet its specification and still slow clinicians down if the requirements missed how care is given.
My approach
Gather needs from clinicians, managers and technical teams, document them clearly, test them through user acceptance testing and manage change as it comes.
What I bring
Business requirements documents, functional specifications, UAT, stakeholder analysis, and Jira and Confluence.
